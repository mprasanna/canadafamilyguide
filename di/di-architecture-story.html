Narrative · The platform in plain language

What happens when a customer
falls through the cracks?

The whole platform as a story — told through one customer, one failing payment, and the question no existing system answers.

Follow ABC Corp through every layer: $24,000 ARR · CRM + Books + Projects · renewal in 15 days · payment failed yesterday · usage down 30% · 3 open support tickets.
Layer 01 · The moment it goes wrong

Yesterday, ABC Corp's payment failed. Nobody noticed.

Store logged the failure. Books recorded the missed invoice. Desk has three open tickets, the oldest fourteen days old. Analytics could chart all of it if someone ran the report. But nobody did — because with thousands of accounts, nobody could.

This is not a data problem. Zoho has all the data. This is a decision problem: which account needs attention right now, what should happen, and who should do it? That question has no system to answer it today.

Layer 02 · One view

Everything comes together — Account 360

First, the pieces are unified into one live profile per customer: products, ARR, renewal date, payment health, usage trend, support risk. For ABC Corp, that's one screen instead of six systems. This already exists.

Extended with a customer's wider business and external context, it becomes Business 360 — the difference between knowing a customer and knowing their business.

Layer 03 · An opinion

The system forms a view — Scores

No human can read millions of profiles, so the platform distils each one into a few 0–100 scores. ABC Corp: Health 45, Churn 78, Payment Risk 92, Expansion 25. Now the system has an opinion, not just data.

Layer 04 · Specialists

Agents recommend — but none of them decides

Think of agents as specialists, not the people running the hospital. The Recovery Agent looks only at payments and says "retry — 85% likely." The Cancellation Agent says "offer monthly — 42%." The Bundle Agent says "Zoho One is cheaper."

Each is an expert in one thing. Crucially, none of them decides its own importance.

Layer 05 · The decision

The brain chooses — the NBA (Next Business Action) Engine

Every recommendation reports to one place. The NBA Engine turns each into a single comparable number — expected value — and ranks them. For ABC Corp, recovering the $24,000 payment far outweighs a long-shot cross-sell, so recovery wins.

"Which action creates the highest business value?" — one question, asked of every customer, every day.
Layer 06 · The guardrails

Rules and limits keep it safe — Policy, Capacity, Budget

The Policy Engine can veto any action no matter how it ranks: no discount over the cap, no cross-sell to an at-risk account. The Capacity Engine only creates as many human tasks as there are people to do them — top 50 of 10,000, not 10,000 ignored. The Communication Budget ensures the customer gets one well-chosen touch, not five at once.

Layer 07 · Act, then learn

Something changes — and the system remembers

The chosen action goes out through the right channel and someone acts. Then, unlike most systems, this one doesn't stop: the Outcome Store records what happened — sent, viewed, accepted, renewed, churned.

The Learning Loop turns those outcomes into sharper future decisions. A save offer that worked 20% of the time learns its way to 37%. The platform gets better on its own.

Store isn't really building agents. It's building a Decision Intelligence platform whose job is to turn information into judgment, judgment into attention, and attention into action — and because Zoho already holds Store, CRM, Books, Desk, Projects, and Analytics, the eventual question isn't just about Store. It's: given everything Zoho knows about a customer and their business, what is the next best action?
