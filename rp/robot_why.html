The decision

Robot architect.
Not cloud architect with robots attached.

Two decades of distributed systems work is already on the résumé. This project exists to prove the part that isn't.

01 — The credibility gap

Nobody doubts the cloud. They doubt the robot.

A hiring manager reading twenty years of platform architecture already believes the mission scheduler, the event bus, the observability stack. Building those again is a demonstration of something that was never in question.

What is in question sits lower in the stack, and it is the reason a fleet-platform portfolio piece would have been the wrong artifact:

  • Coordinate frames, timing, and why odom and map drift apart
  • Sensor noise, calibration as a procedure rather than a word
  • What real-time means when a velocity loop misses its deadline
  • Why safety must be hardware-rated and independent of software state
  • What actually breaks when software meets imperfect hardware
Consequence

The robot stack is the deliverable. The operations layer stays deliberately small — one service, one dashboard page, advisory only, permanently outside the safety and velocity-control paths.

02 — Layer ownership

Every layer from the motor winding to the operator's screen.

The differentiator is not that the robot drives. It is that each layer is derived from requirements, measured, and documented well enough for another engineer to reproduce it.

Physical
Chassis geometry, wheel sizing, weight distribution, power, fusing, wiring, hardware e-stop, sensor mounting
Real-time control
1 kHz velocity loops, quadrature decoding, low-speed velocity estimation, current sensing with I²t protection
ROS 2 integration
C++ hardware interface, controllers, lifecycle, executors and callback groups, explicit QoS
State estimation
Odometry derivation and calibration, IMU bias, timestamping, covariance, REP-103/105 discipline
Autonomy
Mapping, localisation, planners, controllers, behaviour trees, recovery behaviours
Reliability
Diagnostics, watchdogs, bag-driven investigation, failure injection, root cause, safe degraded states
Operations
One telemetry service, mission state, alerts, post-failure log upload. Advisory only.

03 — What gets presented

Three artifacts, not one demo.

The robot attracts attention. The other two make the case, and neither can be produced from tutorials.

Working robot
A real machine completing a repeatable mission in a declared operating domain
Failure reports
Three investigations: symptom, hypothesis, bag evidence, measurement, root cause, correction, regression check
Reference architecture
Control plane versus data plane, real-time boundaries, MCU/host split, lifecycle sequencing, fault containment, how a second robot variant reuses the same platform
Why the third one matters most

Anyone can eventually make a robot drive. Deriving requirements, recording the trade-offs, and writing the platform so the next variant costs weeks instead of months — that is the part twenty years buys, applied to the robot rather than the cloud.

04 — Definition of done

Falsifiable, or it isn't a finish line.

  1. Ten consecutive autonomous goals on hardware, zero manual interventions, final pose within 10 cm and 5°.
  2. Route spans at least 30 m, includes one 180° reversal and one passage no wider than 1.5× the footprint.
  3. The run repeats on a different day from a freshly built map — cross-session repeatability, not one good take.
  4. Simulation and hardware share one controller configuration; only the hardware plugin differs.
  5. Three failures reproduced, measured, diagnosed, corrected and regression-checked, each with bag evidence.
  6. A five-minute recording: normal operation, injected failure, evidence trail, recovery.
  7. Another engineer clones the repository, reproduces the simulation, and reads the architecture without help.

Out of scope, version 1

Manipulator arm. Second physical robot. Isaac Sim digital twin. Kubernetes, Kafka, service mesh. Any language model inside a control path. Each of these is excluded by decision, recorded, and revisited only on evidence.
