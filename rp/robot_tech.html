01 — Drivetrain derivation

The sustained case sets the motor. Not the transient.

At 10 kg, r = 0.045 m, a = 0.5 m/s², 3° incline, rolling resistance 0.03, two driven wheels:

Load cases → force → torque per wheel
Flat floor, constant speed2.94 N0.066 N·m
3° incline, constant speed — sustained worst case8.07 N0.182 N·m
3° incline + full acceleration — transient, ≈1.2 s13.07 N0.294 N·m
0.182N·m sizing figure
0.52N·m thermal ceiling
2.8×margin
0.93N·m traction limit
Why this matters

Acceleration is a 1.2-second event and does not set the thermal limit. Sizing on the transient would have pushed the drivetrain a class larger for no reason — and the earlier 120 mm / 0.8 m/s combination could not be met by this motor class at all.

Selected motor

Motor
37D-class metal gearmotor, 12 V, 70:1 nominal, 64 CPR encoder on the motor shaft. Confirm the exact ratio from the datasheet — it is not exactly 70 — and use that value in the parameter file.
Published limits
Stall 5.5 A, free-run 200 mA. Gearbox continuous 10 kg·cm (0.98 N·m), instantaneous 25 kg·cm (2.45 N·m). Brushed-motor guidance: 25% of stall current or less.
Output constant
≈0.44 N·m per amp at the gearbox output
Thermal ceiling
1.375 A → ≈0.52 N·m continuous. Against 0.182 N·m sustained: ≈2.8× margin.
Expected currents
Flat cruise ≈0.35 A · sustained 3° climb ≈0.61 A · transient on grade ≈0.87 A
Speed check
≈132 rpm at the output under sustained load → 0.62 m/s available. Commanding 0.5 m/s keeps the velocity loop in authority at top speed.
Encoder resolution
64 × 70 = 4,480 counts per wheel revolution over a 283 mm circumference → 63 µm per count. Verify physically with the one-revolution test before trusting odometry.
Traction limit
≈0.93 N·m per wheel at µ = 0.6 with 70% of mass on the drive axle. Below the gearbox instantaneous limit, so a collision slips the wheels rather than damaging the drivetrain — preserve this by not increasing wheel diameter or axle load later.

02 — Power and protection

An oversized driver cannot protect an undersized motor.

  • The driver must provide current sense. A 10 A driver protects nothing on a 1.4 A motor — protection lives in firmware and firmware needs measurement.
  • Firmware limits: 1.375 A continuous per motor, short allowance to 2.5 A for under one second, enforced by an I²t accumulator with a latched fault.
  • Battery: 4S LiFePO4, 12.8 V nominal, 6–10 Ah. The flat discharge curve keeps the velocity feedforward gain stable across a mission.
  • Voltage-compensated feedforward: scale drive output by measured bus voltage, or the gain drifts roughly 20% between a full and an empty pack.
  • Cruise current budget ≈2 A total — motors, onboard computer, LiDAR, microcontroller — giving 3–5 hours.
  • The hardware e-stop removes motor drive power or the driver enable line independently of the microcontroller and of ROS 2. Firmware may observe the state; it must never mediate it.

One architecture end to end.

Development machine
Ryzen mini PC, 680M-class integrated graphics, 32 GB, 1 TB NVMe. Ubuntu 24.04 amd64.
Robot computer
N100-class x86 mini PC. Same distribution and ROS 2 version as development. Bought after base motion tests.
Laptop
Documentation, decision records, git review, the operations service, and SSH into both machines. Not the reference environment — Apple Silicon has no native ROS 2 and no accelerated simulation path.
Reference platform
Ubuntu 24.04 amd64 · ROS 2 Jazzy · Gazebo Harmonic. No ARM anywhere in the loop, so architecture differences never surface as mystery middleware bugs.
Middleware
Default DDS in v1. QoS profiles explicit in code — never left to defaults on sensor or command topics.

Confirm which ROS 2 distribution the target team uses before installing anything. If it is Humble the reference platform becomes Ubuntu 22.04 — a one-line change in week 1 and a rebuild in month six.

04 — Decision register

Eleven records, written at the moment of decision.

Context, options, decision, consequences, and the calculation behind it. This register is the artifact that most directly demonstrates architectural judgement, and it costs almost nothing if written as you go rather than reconstructed later.

Reference platform
001
Ubuntu 24.04 amd64 + ROS 2 Jazzy. One architecture from desk to robot; records the distro check or the open risk.
Operating envelope
002
Indoor level floors, 0.5 m/s commanded, 3° design incline. Speed and gradeability traded for torque margin and control authority.
Drivetrain
003
2WD + caster, 90 mm direct-mount wheels, 70:1 gearing. Direct mounting removes a cantilever bending moment and load-dependent rolling-radius error.
Transport
004
Framed USB serial in v1 behind a transport abstraction; CAN deferred. Bit timing and arbitration debugging would delay first motion by weeks.
Safety
005
Hardware e-stop independent of firmware and ROS 2. Safety must not depend on software state.
IMU handling
006
Raw gyro and accelerometer into the estimator; onboard fusion unused. Known covariance and timestamps; no hidden second estimator.
Motor protection
007
Enforced in firmware; driver chosen for current sense. Enforcement requires measurement.
Perception scope
008
Single 2D LiDAR. The blind plane is documented as an operating-domain limitation, not mitigated in v1.
Language policy
009
C++ for control-adjacent components; Python for tooling and tests.
Cloud scope
010
Advisory only. Never in the safety or velocity-control path; any model explains telemetry and issues no commands.
Fabrication
011
Aluminium extrusion and off-the-shelf brackets. No 3D printer in v1 — fabrication is a dependency, not a new skill to acquire mid-project.
