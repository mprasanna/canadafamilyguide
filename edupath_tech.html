02 / canonical longitudinal memory

One brain. Three kinds of state.

The brain isn't a database. It's an append-only markdown wiki with structured frontmatter — timestamped at ingestion, revision-logged, readable by the student. Every specialist reads from it. None of them writes to it directly except through a documented path.

01 — Structured

Deterministic data

  • grades, by term
  • course selections
  • competition results
  • extracurricular log
  • deadline calendar
  • activity continuity
02 — Narrative

Thematic observations

  • journal themes (no verbatim)
  • interest signals
  • directional continuity
  • peer-context patterns
  • mood trend lines
  • passing-vs-persistent threads
03 — Governance

Operating rules

  • freshness tiers per source
  • confidence values
  • escalation thresholds
  • authority boundaries
  • mode-state log
  • revision history

The third column is what most agent systems leave implicit. Making it first-class state — readable, auditable, timestamped — is what makes the orchestration honest.

03 / the two decision gates

After agents return, two questions are asked in order.

The orchestration's hard part isn't dispatching — it's reconciling outputs that disagree. Mentor uses two gates, in this exact order. The order matters.

Gate 1 Did Time Manager fire a wellbeing signal?
→ Yes Other agents' optimization outputs are softened or suppressed. Wellbeing outranks achievement, always. Hard architectural rule. The system stops trying to improve her week.
→ No Continue to Gate 2.
Gate 2 Did Trajectory's confidence cross the override threshold?
→ Yes Direction vector updates. Structured agents' recommendations get re-anchored to the new direction. The system has, with deliberate constraint, allowed itself to change its mind.
→ No Direction holds. Structured agents continue at their current confidence floor.
Then Route to surfaces.
→ Sage Monthly summary, in her voice. Reflective. Lightweight. No identity claims. No aggressive optimization.
→ Atlas Weekly digest, in calm cadence. Directional summaries. Door-state visibility. Freshness footer. Refuses to choose for her.

Order matters because wellbeing-first must precede direction-update. Otherwise a strong direction signal during a high-overload week would push her further into the overload. The gate sequence is a load-bearing architectural decision.

04 / a canonical run

The Grade 10 January moment, traced end-to-end.

Same event, from input to output

Two signals from two columns. One Sunday digest. Twenty minutes of agent time.

Stage 1. Mid-January report card arrives via my Atlas form. Math 82. The thematic summarizer's month-end run on her PWA already produced a record three weeks earlier — "positive engagement with visual/spatial work, school context, three sources."
Stage 2. The grade lands in structured state, timestamped, fresh.
Stage 3. Mentor classifies: report card event + course selection window two months away. Dispatches scoped state to Academic Reader, Curriculum Tracker, Interest Trajectory, and Time Manager.
Stage 4. In parallel: Academic Reader sees a second-term watch — alert escalating. Curriculum Tracker recomputes door state: Waterloo competitive entry now marginal at math 82, industrial design widens. Trajectory reads the visual/spatial signal now at three sources, crosses confidence 0.6. Time Manager reports no overload.
Stage 5. Mentor's reconciliation. Gate 1: no wellbeing suppression. Gate 2: trajectory confidence has crossed 0.5 but not the override threshold (persistence still only 5 months). Direction holds; Trajectory's read is surfaced as signal, not yet as override. Mentor detects the cross-column pair — direction shifting, doors narrowing — and surfaces it.
Stage 6. Composes for Atlas under the canonical-phrase guide. The Sunday digest reads: "Trajectory signal has shifted with confidence 0.6 toward visual/spatial work. Math at 82, trending downward. The right time to revisit Direction is now." Not "she is drifting away from engineering." Never that.
