Eleven agents, two surfaces, one privacy boundary. A five-minute tour of the architecture I built for my daughter.

Not pitching anything. A father's project, shared because the thinking might be of interest.

01 / why this exists

The system optimizes for her optionality, not her admission.

An earlier draft of this architecture treated specific universities as targets. I scrapped it. A fixed goal at 13 is a hypothesis, not a destination. The cultural pattern of engineering-parent ambition optimization is exactly what good architecture should resist, not encode.

The corrected framing — and the load-bearing one — is to fix the direction and the constraints, not the destination. Direction is captured as a vector across four dimensions, not as a label. "Robotics" is a label and forbidden. "Building × technical × collaborative" is a vector and useful.

Bespoke per-student brain

Mentor's brain is a markdown wiki specific to her. Append-only. Timestamped. Revision-logged. Not a profile inferred from behavior — a record co-authored with her.

Freshness as foundation

Every observation timestamped. Every output footed with the provenance of its inputs. Staleness escalates through three tiers and pauses agents at the top tier.

The privacy boundary is real

Enforced at the filesystem level, not by prompt instructions. Her conversation content does not surface to me. This is the architectural commitment everything else depends on.

The lifecycle ends

When she starts university, active orchestration shuts down. The four-year trajectory archive becomes hers. The system was never supposed to be permanent.

03 / the privacy boundary

Two surfaces. One boundary. Enforced at the filesystem level.

Both surfaces drink from the same Mentor brain, but with different scopes. Sage sees verbatim. Atlas sees aggregated. The architectural commitment is that the boundary cannot be bypassed by clever prompting — Sage's agent process literally cannot write to anywhere Atlas can read from.

Sage

student-facing — scoped to her

Conversational. Real-time. Warm older-cousin tone, calibrated to not-preachy and not-inquisitive. Sage hears everything. Sage tells me almost nothing.

  • Sees verbatim journal entries
  • Sees named relationships and friends
  • Sees emotional content as it unfolds
  • Holds private notes she redacts at will
  • Can be paused by her without negotiation

Atlas

parent-facing — that's me

Weekly digest. Event-triggered alerts. Annual review. Boring on purpose. Atlas is my window into thematic patterns — not into what she said.

  • Sees aggregated thematic patterns only
  • Sees no verbatim content, ever
  • Sees no names of friends or peers
  • Sees no emotional content below threshold
  • Refuses to make decisions on her behalf
Canonical refusal

"The system can't choose for her, and shouldn't. Here's the door map. Here's what Trajectory has been signaling. She's not ready to decide yet, and that's appropriate at this stage."

04 / freshness as foundation

Every output carries its own provenance.

A system that confidently reports stale data lies. The freshness layer was the first thing built — before any agent, before either surface. Every observation is timestamped at ingestion. Every output is footed with the age and tier of each input it consumed. Staleness escalates through three tiers: fresh, aging, stale. At the top tier, the relevant agent pauses with an explicit "operating on stale context" notice rather than continuing silently.

05 / four years, stress-tested

I ran the architecture against a fictional student before writing any code.

The simulation walked a 13-year-old through Grades 9-12 and surfaced nine architectural failure modes the original design didn't handle — moments where confidence inflated, where the privacy boundary almost leaked, where Atlas's language slipped toward identity-claim phrasing. Each failure required a specific revision, which is now incorporated into the philosophy commitments.

06 / what gets left behind

When the system shuts down, she keeps everything.

At university entry, active orchestration ends. The system produces one final artifact and delivers it to her — not to me. This may be the single most emotionally meaningful output the architecture produces. I don't think most agent design considers what gets left behind when an agent's job ends.

The trajectory archive — four years of her own becoming, in her own words and observations.

An append-only, timestamped record. Direction-vector snapshots at each mode review. Every annual review. Every door-state map. The revision log of the Direction + Constraints document showing how her family's thinking evolved alongside hers. The external reviewer's notes, year by year. Explicit failed-interest entries — the things she tried, didn't stick with, and what she learned from each.
